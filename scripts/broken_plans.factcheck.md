# Fact-check: Why Do We Make Plans We Never KEEP?

Every fact scene, its source, how it was checked and how the wording was kept honest. Checked 2026-11-09.

## Method
- Every study was found online and its abstract, record or a reliable summary opened; contested points are told as such.
- No health or professional advice; the if-then tip is told as what studies tested, with the smaller 2024 estimate said out loud.

## Sources and checks

### Sydney Opera House: planned about 1963 at about A$7 million, opened 1973 at about A$102 million (Flyvbjerg; Classical Music; Architecture & Design)
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-09; ArchDaily, Classical Music, Architecture & Design and Planisware histories: estimate about A$7 million and completion around 1963; opened 20 October 1973; final cost about A$102 million (about 14-15x); roof shells forced rebuilds; Utzon resigned 1966.
- Wording safeguard: 'About ten years late', 'roughly fourteen times the budget'.

### Buehler, Griffin & Ross (1994). Exploring the 'planning fallacy'. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 67, 366-381
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-09; paper PDF (UF course copy) and summaries: 37 students; thesis predicted 33.9 days, took 55.5 days; about 30% finished within their estimate; the average 'worst case' (48.6 days) was also too short; outsiders predicting others' tasks are more pessimistic.
- Wording safeguard: 'About thirty percent', 'in one classic study'.

### Webb & Sheeran (2006). Does changing behavioral intentions engender behavior change? Psychological Bulletin 132, 249-268
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-09; meta-analysis records and Sheeran & Webb 2016 review: 47 experiments; a medium-to-large change in intention (d = .66) led to a small-to-medium change in behaviour (d = .36).
- Wording safeguard: Said as 'a big change in intention bought only a small change in action'; the popular '47% of intenders fail' line is NOT used.

### Hershfield et al. (2011). Neural measures of future self-continuity predict temporal discounting (Stanford/UCLA fMRI study)
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-09; UCLA Health release and Boston Globe/NYU Stern coverage: brain activity when describing yourself in ten years looked less like describing your current self and more like describing another person (actors); people with the biggest difference chose small immediate rewards more often. Small Stanford sample.
- Wording safeguard: 'Looked more like thinking about someone else', 'in a small study'.

### Steel, P. (2007). The nature of procrastination: a meta-analytic and theoretical review. Psychological Bulletin 133, 65-94
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-09; Steel 2007 text: procrastination chronically affects some 15-20% of adults (citing Harriott & Ferrari 1996). The 80-95% student figure rests on old sources and is not used.
- Wording safeguard: 'Roughly one in six to one in five adults'.

### Norcross & Vangarelli (1988-89). The resolution solution: longitudinal examination of New Year's change attempts. J. Substance Abuse
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-09; Oscarsson 2020 literature review describing Norcross & Vangarelli's 200 resolvers: about 40% still successful after six months.
- Wording safeguard: 'About four in ten after six months'; the viral '92% fail' figure is not used (no study found).

### Oscarsson et al. (2020). A large-scale experiment on New Year's resolutions. PLOS ONE 15, e0234097
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-09; PLOS ONE paper (PMC7725288): 1,066 participants; approach goals ('start doing') 58.9% successful vs avoidance goals ('stop doing') 47.1% after one year.
- Wording safeguard: Exact percentages, framed as one large experiment.

### Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006). Implementation intentions and goal achievement: a meta-analysis. Adv. Exp. Social Psychology 38; Sheeran et al. (2024) update
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-09; meta-analysis summaries: 94 independent tests, about 8,000 people, medium-to-large effect (d = .65); a 2024 meta-analysis finds a smaller average effect (d about .27-.36 for behaviour).
- Wording safeguard: 'Worked across dozens of studies', the smaller 2024 estimate is said out loud; no 'doubles your success' claim.

### Milne, Orbell & Sheeran (2002). Combining motivational and volitional interventions to promote exercise. British Journal of Health Psychology 7, 163-184
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-09; summaries of the study: 248 UK students; of those who also wrote when and where they would exercise, 91% reported doing it within two weeks; self-report. Control-group figure not verified, so not used.
- Wording safeguard: 'Ninety-one percent said they did it', self-report mentioned.

## Claims softened or dropped
- '47% of intenders fail to act': not found in any source, dropped; 'intentions rose a lot, behaviour about half as much' used (d .66 vs .36).
- '92% of resolutions fail': no study found, dropped; Norcross 'about 40% after six months' used.
- '80-95% of students procrastinate': rests on old non-measured sources, dropped; '15-20% of adults chronic' used.
- Milne 2002 comparison groups (often quoted 38%/35%): not verified, dropped; only the 91% self-report is said.
- 'If-then plans double success': not claimed; 'worked across dozens of studies' plus the smaller 2024 estimate.
- Fresh-start effect (Dai et al. 2014): not verified in this search, dropped.
