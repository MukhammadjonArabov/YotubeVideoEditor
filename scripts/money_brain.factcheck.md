# Fact-check: Why Is Saving MONEY So Hard for Humans?

Every fact scene, its source, how it was checked and how the wording was kept honest. Checked 2026-11-02.

## Method
- Every study was found online and its abstract, record or a reliable summary opened; contested points are told as such.
- No financial advice: the video explains why brains struggle with money and never tells viewers what to buy, save or invest.
- Modern foragers are not treated as living ancestors; 'many foragers' and 'anthropologists describe' are used.

## Sources and checks

### Klapper, Lusardi & van Oudheusden (2015). Financial Literacy Around the World: S&P Global FinLit Survey (2014 Gallup data)
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-02; GFLEC presentation and trade-press summaries of the S&P Global FinLit Survey: questions in the Gallup World Poll, 150,000+ adults in 140+ countries, 33% of adults worldwide financially literate (3 of 4 concepts right: interest, compounding, inflation, diversification); US 57%.
- Wording safeguard: 'About one in three passed', the test is called 'simple money questions'; the year is not hidden (description says 2014 data).

### Hublin et al. (2017). New fossils from Jebel Irhoud, Morocco and the pan-African origin of Homo sapiens. Nature 546, 289-292
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-02 (well-known Nature paper): Jebel Irhoud fossils dated about 300,000 years.
- Wording safeguard: 'About three hundred thousand years'.

### Woodburn, J. (1982). Egalitarian societies. Man 17(3), 431-451
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-02; secondary summaries (P2P Foundation, UCL chapter) of Woodburn's immediate-return model based on Hadza fieldwork: food consumed soon after it is obtained, little storage, demand sharing. A later study found big game sharing favours the hunter's household.
- Wording safeguard: 'Anthropologists describe', 'many foragers'; not claimed for every prehistoric group.

### McClure, Laibson, Loewenstein & Cohen (2004). Separate neural systems value immediate and delayed monetary rewards. Science 306, 503-507
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-02; Princeton and ASU records and abstract: fMRI, choices between money now and later; limbic/paralimbic areas more active for immediate rewards, lateral prefrontal and parietal areas for all choices; more fronto-parietal activity when people chose to wait. Later replications are mixed.
- Wording safeguard: 'Brain scans suggest', 'one famous study'; no claim that a single 'impulse centre' exists.

### Kahneman & Tversky (1979). Prospect theory. Econometrica 47; Tversky & Kahneman (1992). Advances in prospect theory. J. Risk and Uncertainty 5
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-02; Wikipedia summary and critiques (Jason Collins blog, Yechiam): prospect theory 1979 introduced loss aversion; the about-twice estimate (2 to 2.25) comes from 1992 work; a 2020 multi-country replication broadly supported the 1979 patterns; strength of loss aversion is debated.
- Wording safeguard: 'Roughly twice', 'for many people', and the debate is mentioned.

### Prelec & Simester (2001). Always leave home without it: the credit-card effect on willingness to pay. Marketing Letters 12, 5-12
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-02; MIT faculty page, MSU Extension and Psychology Today summaries: MIT MBA students bid on Celtics tickets; those allowed to pay by card bid roughly twice as much as cash-only bidders.
- Wording safeguard: 'In one MIT experiment', 'roughly double'; not claimed that cards always double spending.

### Luttmer, E. (2005). Neighbors as negatives: relative earnings and well-being. Quarterly Journal of Economics 120, 963-1002
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-02; NBER working paper w10667 abstract: matched panel data; higher neighbours' earnings associated with lower self-reported happiness, holding own income constant.
- Wording safeguard: 'Linked to', 'on average'.

### Code of Hammurabi (c. 1754 BCE), laws on loans and interest; Mesopotamian loan tablets (silver about 20%, grain about 33%)
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-02; Burr & Forman legal-history article and popular histories: Mesopotamian loans in silver and barley, commonly cited rates about 20% on silver and 33% on grain, Code of Hammurabi regulated loans (c. 1754 BCE); proto-cuneiform accounting tablets from about 3100 BCE. Weak blog sources ignored.
- Wording safeguard: 'About a fifth', 'about a third', 'around'; no exact dates beyond 'nearly four thousand years ago' for Hammurabi and 'over five thousand years' for writing-based accounts.

### Wiessner & Huang (2022). Ju/'hoansi kinship obligations, cash and risk pooling, 1974-2018. PNAS 119, e2213214119
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-02; PNAS 2022 abstract (PMC9564820), Utah news release and Wiessner's 1977 dissertation title: hxaro gift partners as far as about 200 km; in 1974 about 69% of possessions had come as hxaro gifts; partnerships fell by about half by 1997 as cash spread.
- Wording safeguard: 'Up to about two hundred kilometres', 'in the nineteen seventies', 'about seven in ten'.

### Watts, Duncan & Quan (2018). Revisiting the marshmallow test. Psychological Science 29, 1159-1177
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-02; Psychological Science abstract and summaries: about 900 children (NICHD SECCYD), link with later achievement about half the original size and reduced by about two thirds after family background controls.
- Wording safeguard: 'Much smaller', 'family background mattered a lot'; willpower not called useless.

## Claims softened or dropped
- 'Losses hurt exactly 2x': the factor comes from later estimates and is debated; said as 'roughly twice', debate mentioned.
- FinLit 33% is 2014 data; said as 'about one in three passed', year in the description.
- Mesopotamian interest rates come from legal-history summaries; said as 'about a fifth' and 'about a third'.
- Woodburn's immediate-return model rests on Hadza fieldwork; a later study found big game sharing favours the hunter's household; 'many foragers' used.
- McClure 2004 limbic finding: later replications mixed; 'brain scans suggest' and the debate line used.
- Prelec & Simester: one MIT experiment; 'in one experiment' said, no general claim that cards double spending.
- 'Knowing a bias is the first step to noticing it' is a general framing, not a cited effect size.
- Dropped: the claim that lottery winners usually go broke (no solid source found).
