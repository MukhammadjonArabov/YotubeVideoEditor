# Fact-check: Why Were Rainy Nights So DEADLY for Ancient Humans?

Every fact scene, its source, how it was checked and how the wording was kept honest. Checked 2026-10-26.

## Method
- Topic and structure inspired by Before Civilization's top video (rainy nights, about 466k views); no wording copied.
- Living foragers and modern safety guidance are used as windows, and the narration says so where it matters.
- Not survival or medical advice; the description says so.

## Sources and checks

### Parmeggiani (1960s onward), reviewed in Frontiers in Physiology (2017), 'Sleep and thermoregulation' (shivering and panting are suppressed in REM sleep; mostly animal data)
- How verified: Searched 2026-10-26; Frontiers in Physiology 2017 review and PubMed 7166740 (human REM sweating study). Shivering and panting are suppressed in REM sleep; first described by Parmeggiani; most evidence from cats and rats, human data limited.
- Wording safeguard: 'Your body's heating system mostly switches off', 'studies, mostly in animals'; no human number given.

### Princeton University Outdoor Action, 'Hypothermia and cold weather injuries' (water conducts heat about 25 times faster than air; a field-guide rule of thumb)
- How verified: Searched 2026-10-26; Princeton Outdoor Action page and US safety guides repeat 'about 25 times'; it compares thermal conductivity and is a rule of thumb, not a body-heat measurement.
- Wording safeguard: 'About twenty-five times', called a rule of thumb; no 'wet clothes = five times' claim.

### Kjellberg Grahn et al. (2015). Protection against cold in prehospital care: wet clothing removal or addition of a vapor barrier. Wilderness & Environmental Medicine 26, 11-20
- How verified: Searched 2026-10-26; Wilderness & Environmental Medicine 2015 abstract: eight volunteers in wet clothing at minus 18.5 C; removing wet clothing or adding a vapour barrier lowered shivering heat production and improved skin rewarming; core temperature did not differ.
- Wording safeguard: Only 'less shivering, skin warmed better'; core temperature result stated; eight volunteers mentioned.

### US National Weather Service and NOAA flash-flood guidance (dry washes can flood from rain falling upstream; 2017 Cold Springs Canyon flood near Payson, Arizona)
- How verified: Searched 2026-10-26; NOAA photo captions and NWS warnings: dry washes become dangerous when storms are upstream; flash floods can come from rain that is not overhead. Cold Springs Canyon 2017: about 1.5 inches of rain fell upstream; several people swimming downstream died (reports give nine or ten).
- Wording safeguard: 'Several people', 'rain that fell miles upstream'; no exact death toll.

### Riel-Salvatore et al., Riparo Bombrini rock shelter, Italy (an artefact gap marks the old drip line; hearths and debris outside it)
- How verified: Searched 2026-10-26; Riparo Bombrini summary (Riel-Salvatore et al.): a gap in artefacts across levels is read as the former drip line; hearths and debris accumulated outside it. Site reports elsewhere describe hearths just inside drip lines.
- Wording safeguard: 'Archaeologists read a gap', interpretation, not a rule for every cave.

### Wadley et al. (2020). Fire and grass-bedding construction 200 thousand years ago at Border Cave, South Africa. Science 369, 863-866
- How verified: Searched 2026-10-26; Wits University release, Inside Science, Sci.News: grass bedding at least 200,000 years old near the back of Border Cave, laid on ash; camphor bush remains on the oldest bedding; insect-repelling purpose is the authors' suggestion.
- Wording safeguard: 'At least two hundred thousand years', 'the researchers think' for the ash and camphor purpose.

### Wadley et al. (2011). Middle Stone Age bedding construction and settlement patterns at Sibudu, South Africa. Science 334, 1388-1391
- How verified: Searched 2026-10-26; ScienceDaily, National Geographic, NBC: Sibudu bedding in at least 15 layers, oldest about 77,000 years, sedges and rushes topped with Cryptocarya woodii leaves whose chemicals can kill or repel insects such as mosquitoes; bedding periodically burned.
- Wording safeguard: 'About seventy-seven thousand', 'leaves with insect-killing chemicals'; the mosquito purpose is an inference.

### Nadel et al. (2004). Stone Age hut in Israel yields world's oldest evidence of bedding. PNAS 101, 6821-6826
- How verified: Searched 2026-10-26; University of Haifa record and PNAS summaries: about 23,000-year-old brush huts on the Sea of Galilee shore with grass bedding around a central hearth; 'oldest' claim later superseded by Sibudu and Border Cave.
- Wording safeguard: 'About twenty-three thousand years'; not called the oldest.

## Claims softened or dropped
- 'Wet clothes make you lose heat five times faster': only from field guides, dropped.
- Raised sleeping platforms and drainage trenches: no reliable archaeological source opened, dropped.
- Ash and leaves against insects: told as the researchers' interpretation.
- REM shivering: 'mostly animal studies' said out loud.
- Cold Springs Canyon death toll: 'several' (reports give nine or ten).
- 'Twenty-five times': called a rule of thumb about heat conduction.
- Night fire-keeping and sleeping order: stated for living foragers in general, not as measured ancient facts.
