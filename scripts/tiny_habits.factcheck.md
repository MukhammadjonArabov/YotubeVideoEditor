# Fact-check: Why Tiny Daily Habits Beat MOTIVATION

Every fact scene, its source, how it was checked and how the wording was kept honest. Checked 2026-11-10.

## Method
- Every study was found online and its abstract, record or a reliable summary opened; limits and debates are said out loud.
- No health advice: habits are examples (water, walking), never treatment.

## Sources and checks

### Arithmetic illustration: 1.01^365 is about 37.8 and 0.99^365 is about 0.03 (a comparison, not a study)
- How verified: Computed: 1.01^365 = 37.78, 0.99^365 = 0.026.
- Wording safeguard: Said as 'just math', 'a way to picture it', not as a finding.

### Wood, Quinn & Kashy (2002). Habits in everyday life. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 83, 1281-1297; Wood & Rünger (2016)
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-10; paper PDF on USC site and textbook summaries: hourly beeper diaries from 279 students; about 41% of behaviours habitual (same time and place); later reviews: about 43-47% of everyday actions repeated almost daily in the same place.
- Wording safeguard: 'About four in ten', 'close to half', no exact single number.

### Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle (2010). How are habits formed: modelling habit formation in the real world. European Journal of Social Psychology 40, 998-1009
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-10; BPS Research Digest, University of Surrey interview with Lally, behavioural-science summaries: 96 volunteers, one daily behaviour for 84 days; time to reach automaticity averaged about 66 days, range 18 to a modelled 254 days; early repetitions gave the biggest gains; missing one opportunity did not materially affect habit formation.
- Wording safeguard: 'About sixty-six days on average', 'up to around eight months, by the researchers' estimate', 'one study'.

### Hagger et al. (2016). A multilab preregistered replication of the ego-depletion effect. Perspectives on Psychological Science 11, 546-573
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-10; paper records and Discover coverage: 23 labs, 2,141 participants, effect d = 0.04 (95% CI includes zero) vs d = 0.62 in the earlier meta-analysis; debate continues about other task versions.
- Wording safeguard: 'Close to zero in this test', 'scientists still argue'.

### Hofmann, Baumeister, Förster & Vohs (2012). Everyday temptations: an experience sampling study of desire, conflict and self-control. JPSP 102, 1318-1335
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-10; JPSP paper (UMN PDF) and Chicago Booth summary: 205 adults with phones reporting desires for a week; high self-control people had as many desires but felt less conflict and resisted less often, apparently by avoiding tempting situations.
- Wording safeguard: 'As many desires', 'resisted less often', 'seem to avoid'.

### Galla & Duckworth (2015). More than resisting temptation: beneficial habits mediate the relationship between self-control and positive life outcomes. JPSP 109, 508-525
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-10; PMC4731333 and abstracts: across six studies, beneficial habits mediated the link between self-control and good outcomes (exercise, study, meditation).
- Wording safeguard: 'Mostly through habits', 'in these studies'.

### Amabile & Kramer (2011). The Progress Principle. Harvard Business Review Press (about 12,000 diary entries from 238 people)
- How verified: Searched 2026-11-10; HBS Working Knowledge and summaries: about 12,000 daily diary entries from 238 professionals in 26 teams; progress in meaningful work was the most common feature of their best days; most events were small.
- Wording safeguard: 'Small progress', 'at work', the professional sample is noted in the fact-check.

### Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006). Implementation intentions and goal achievement. Advances in Experimental Social Psychology 38
- How verified: See broken_plans.factcheck.md (video #37); mentioned in one line only.
- Wording safeguard: Points back to the earlier video.

## Claims softened or dropped
- '21 days to form a habit': no scientific basis found; used only as the myth that is tested.
- Lally's 254-day upper figure is a statistical projection beyond the 84-day study; said as 'around eight months, by the researchers' estimate'.
- 'Exactly 43% of behaviour is habit': sources give 41-47%; 'about four in ten', 'close to half' used.
- Ego depletion: the 2016 replication used one task version; 'scientists still argue about other versions' is said.
- '1% better every day = 37x': arithmetic only, told as a picture, not a finding.
- Amabile & Kramer: professional workers; 'at work' kept in the narration.
